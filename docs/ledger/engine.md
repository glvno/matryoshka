# Engine

IDs: D-350–D-399

There are two audio engines: the TypeScript **reference engine** in the browser and its port to **fixed point** in the FM-1 firmware, kept in step by **golden kits** and shared parity rules, and a speed-up must be bit-exact. Both run at the FM-1's rate of 44118 Hz in 128-frame blocks. The FM-1 runs the **device form** of a patch, trimmed by a compile pass before encoding, skips **idle parts**, and sheds parts when the audio overloads. Poly assemblies sum their cards where cables leave them, and **paraphonic** ones mix each card at 1 / cards. The engine clock runs from Main's tempo unless MIDI clock drives it, and key events from Arpeggiators, Steps tracks and Drum maps land at the start of the next block. A patch that uses a [**browser-only ability**](vocabulary.md#browser-only-ability) counts as over the FM-1 budget. The editor shows a worst-case CPU estimate fitted to the 17 factory patches measured on the FM-1, and every module type needs a cost in that model.

| <a id="D-370"></a>D-370 | The Follower module (`esp`) plays the role of the Korg MS-20's External Signal Processor (ESP) inside a patch. It takes one signal in. Its controls are the ESP's: signal level, low cut, high cut, control voltage (CV) adjust and threshold. Its outputs are the ESP's: the amplified signal, the band-passed signal, a pitch, an envelope and a trigger. The pitch is in octaves from C4, like every pitch signal in the patch, so it plays an oscillator directly. CV adjust shifts it by up to 12 semitones either way. The pitch comes from timing the band-passed signal's cycles, so it follows one note at a time. It holds its last value when the signal is near silence. The trigger turns on when the envelope passes the threshold and off below three quarters of it. Its band pass is gentle, 6 decibels per octave on each side, and its envelope times are fixed. Its circuit is not reproduced, only its controls and outputs. Sallen 20 has one. The Follower's input is a Sallen 20 input. Its outputs are Sallen 20 outputs. Sallen 20 also has the three inputs the MS-20 manual patches them into: the keyboard pitch of both oscillators, the trigger of both envelopes, and the amplifier's initial gain. The FM-1 has no audio input, so on the FM-1 the Follower hears only signals made in the patch. Sallen 20's card has two more ESP rows, so that every ESP row works on the FM-1. SIGNAL IN picks what the Follower hears: off, VCO 1, the white noise or the MG's triangle. In the browser, a cable into `esp signal in` adds to that choice. ESP ROUTE patches one output as the MS-20 manual does: off, F-V CV into VCO 2's pitch, ENV into the amplifier's initial gain, or TRIG into both envelopes' trigger. F-V goes to VCO 2 alone, as the MS-20's VCO 2 CV IN, because VCO 1 would otherwise follow its own pitch. Both rows default to off, and Sallen 20 then sounds exactly as without them. | Decided | 2026-10-27 | michael glaviano, ruled in the 1.5 review page 2026-10-27; issue #375; issue #303; issue #274; [Korg MS-20 owner's manual (1978), External Signal Processor and Using the External Signal Processor](https://archive.org/details/JL11275); `web/src/modules.ts`; `firmware/src/engine.c`; `web/scripts/gen-assemblies.ts` |

- Whether a preset holding a browser-only value (a Delay time past 520 ms) may still be sent to the FM-1, which plays it at 520 ms — PR #269

- Proposed amendment to [D-365](engine.md#D-365): use the measured 85% shedding threshold for the CPU budget and refit the model against the current factory patches. [The Io 4 survey](../measurements/2026-10-26-io-4-player-budget.json) shows that a modeled fit can still shed a voice. Factory generation keeps Io 4's existing arpeggiator without adding PATTERN while that discrepancy remains.

- Proposed clarification of [D-363](engine.md#D-363): isolated engine behavior uses fixture patches, while factory fidelity tests exercise the generated factory patches and their actual cards. The existing factory audio comparisons and the Monolith D waveform checks exercise the latter.

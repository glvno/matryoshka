# Spike #1338: a DX7 module on Dexed's engine (msfa). Flash, RAM, CPU and patch size

Written 2026-11-26/27 by DexedSpike, for issue #1338 (from #1220). Nothing here is merged. The code lives on the
throwaway branch `spike/dexed-1338` (never merged): `spike/dexed/` holds the core, the tools and the firmware hook, and
`.github/workflows/dexed-spike.yml` builds it with the JieLi toolchain. Its CI run is 45939661320. Local outputs are in
`build/dexed-spike/`: `ci3/` (the CI artifact), `rig/` (blocktime JSON), `out/` (host renders and rv32 runs) and
`ref/` (Dexed built unchanged, as the reference).

## Verdict

**Go on CPU and fidelity. Flash is the one gate: no-go on today's 14 KB unless space is reclaimed.**

- **CPU:** on the test rig, one DX7 voice (6 operators) costs **115.9 µs per 128-sample block**. That is about 41,700 cycles
  at 360 MHz, or 4.0 % of a block. The cost is linear from 1 to 8 voices (fit residuals within ±1.5 µs). Eight voices
  beside Monolith D bring the audio interrupt to 1,784 µs, which is 61 % of a block. One msfa voice costs less than one
  Algo Four voice (about 163 µs for its 4 operators on 1.28.1).
- **Fidelity:** with msfa's own tables the FM-1 cut is bit-identical to Dexed on the ROM voices tried. With the tables
  shared with the firmware, it differs by at most 0.0016 cents and 0.000008 dB.
- **Flash:** the voice core alone links in at **+10,104 B** (msfa's code, the tables shared with the firmware, -O2). A
  whole module needs about 17 to 21 KB (estimated below). The slot has 14,364 B free. So it fits only with an owner
  ruling on one of SpaceReclaim's items. The wavetable mips change (25,600 B) would cover it with room to spare.
- **RAM:** 724 B per voice and about 1.1 KB per module, taken from the engine's arena like any module's. The RAM region
  has no room for it.

## Method

1. **Source.** Dexed `Source/msfa` at c314161a (2026-11-25), Apache-2.0. It was cut to a DX7 voice, and every change is
   marked `FM-1:` in the source:
   - The tables are const, generated by `spike/dexed/gen_tables.py` for 44,118 Hz. Dexed works them out at start-up.
   - No tuning, MTS-ESP, MPE, Logger, `shared_ptr`, `virtual` or `iostream`.
   - The double-precision expressions became integer ones: the detune and amplitude-modulation `exp()` go through
     Exp2, and the fine ratio is a 100-entry table. The pitch bend divides by `12.0f`.
   - `unit.cc` is the smallest synth unit. It does what Dexed's `PluginProcessor` does around the voices: one LFO,
     the controllers, one FmCore, the 64-sample render twice per FM-1 block, and Dexed's clip per voice. It also holds
     msfa's `UnpackPatch`.
2. **Fidelity.** Dexed was built unchanged on the host with stubs for JUCE, MTS and Tunings (`build/dexed-spike/ref`). It
   rendered the same notes as the FM-1 cut (`host_render.cc`, `out/cmp.py`), and `out/tabacc.py` compared the shared
   tables with msfa's over 200,000 inputs.
3. **Flash.** Each object was built with the JieLi clang 4.0.1 and the firmware's flags (`-target pi32v2`, the r3 FPU,
   `-ffunction-sections -fno-builtin`), at -Os and -O2, with msfa's own tables and with the shared ones. Sizes come
   from `spike/dexed/elfsize.py`. Then whole firmware images were built with and without the voices (`DXS_SPIKE=1`).
4. **CPU, model.** `spike/dexed/rv32_bench.cc` ran on `tools/cpu/rv32.mjs`, the cycle model `web/src/cpu-costs.json` is
   counted on.
5. **CPU, rig.** The `DXS_SPIKE=1` firmware plays one voice of a busy test voice for every key the engine hears. All six
   operators sound and are modulated, with operator 6 feedback and the LFO on pitch and amplitude. The voices render
   in the audio interrupt after `eng_render`, so their time shows in blocktime's "Output modules' sum" span. The
   firmware passed `emu:boot` on its exact bytes (49 checks). It was installed on the rig (0x01140000) and read with
   `tools/m1_link.py --rig blocktime 3 --notes N --patch …`, with 12 s between windows so that the last window's
   releases had ended. Then 1.28.1 was reinstalled and measured again in the same session
   ([D-1165](../../docs/ledger/releases.md#D-1165)).

## CPU

### Rig, 2026-11-27 (blocktime, mean per 128-sample half, µs)

Monolith D is monophonic, so its own cost does not depend on the number of notes held. The span that grows is the msfa
render.

| msfa voices | msfa span (settled series) | whole audio interrupt | % of the 2,902 µs block |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 32.3 | 773.5 | 26.7 |
| 1 | 179.3 | 973.1 | 33.5 |
| 2 | 297.9 | 1,094.7 | 37.7 |
| 3 | 412.5 | 1,207.4 | 41.6 |
| 4 | 529.7 | 1,330.4 | 45.8 |
| 6 | 760.5 | 1,555.7 | 53.6 |
| 8 | 991.5 | 1,784.4 | 61.5 |

- **Linear fit (1 to 8 voices): 115.9 µs per voice, plus 65 µs once any voice plays.** Residuals are within ±1.5 µs.
- **With no voice playing**, the hook's loop over its 8 empty voices costs about 20 µs: the span was 32.3 µs, against
  12.5 to 16.8 µs on 1.28.1.
- **On 1.28.1 in the same session**, Monolith D's interrupt was 744 to 834 µs. Algo Four's was 506 to 548 µs with no
  notes and 1,812 to 1,846 µs with 8 notes, so about **163 µs per Algo Four voice** (4 operators, both cores).
- **Beside Algo Four with 8 notes**, the 8 msfa voices' span was 976 µs, the same as beside Monolith D. The whole
  interrupt averaged 2,463 µs (84.9 %) and peaked at 2,574 µs. A later `cpu` read gave a peak of 3,607 µs (124 %),
  with no overload. Algo Four's own voices measured 1,436 µs, against 1,754 µs on 1.28.1. [INFERENCE] The overload
  guard shed some of its voices. So 8 + 8 is at the edge, as expected for two full 8-voice FM engines.
- **How many voices fit.** Beside Monolith D, about 14 voices fit under the 85 % bar: (2,466 − 773 − 65) / 115.9. On an
  otherwise idle engine, using `cpu-costs.json`'s fixed 270 µs per block, about 18 voices fit, all on core 0.
  [INFERENCE] Running the voices in the engine's lanes on both cores would add more.

### Which tables dominate, and the options

- **msfa's own sine and exp2 tables are 8,192 B each, and its frequency table is 4,100 B.** Together they are 85 % of
  its rodata. The firmware's `SIN_T` (sin in Q30, 1,025 entries) and `EXP2_T` (2^(i/256) in Q28, 257 entries)
  replace all three. The frequency table is 2^(i/1024) times a constant, so `EXP2_T` gives it too. That saves
  **20,484 B**. The cost in accuracy:
  - sine: within 2 LSB of msfa's Q24 values (both are about 80 LSB from an exact sine);
  - exp2: 0.000008 dB;
  - frequency: 0.0016 cents.

  On the ROM voices, renders differ only by phase drift, at −26 to −93 dB below the signal. That is not a quality loss.
- **What is left is about 1.8 KB of tables:**

  | table | bytes | smaller form |
  |---|---:|---|
  | algorithms (`int ops[6]`) | 768 | as bytes: 192 (−576) |
  | porta rates and glissando rates | 512 each | the glissando table is the rates × 1300/2100, so one table (−512) |
  | the fine-ratio table | 400 | |
  | the LFO increments | 400 | |
  | the envelope's statics | 308 | |
  | small curves | about 1 KB | |

  Those two changes take the core to about 8.6 KB at -O2, or 7.6 KB at -Os.
- **Q16 conversion: no.** msfa is Q24 inside its loops. Converting it would save no bytes, would cost exactness, and the
  module would still convert only at its output, which is one shift. Signals between modules stay Q16 as today.
- **Port it to C.** The firmware is one C translation unit (`modular.c`). The core uses no C++ beyond classes, so a C
  port is mechanical. The spike linked C++ objects instead, which needed a `build.py` change; a module should not.

## RAM

| what | bytes | where |
|---|---:|---|
| one voice (`Dx7Note`: 6 envelopes, 6 operator states, pitch envelope, feedback, porta) | **724** | engine arena |
| one module: FmCore's two 64-sample buses (544), the 64-sample voice buffer (256), LFO (28), controllers (≈ 100), the 156-byte voice | ≈ 1,100 | engine arena (the buses could share the engine's scratch) |
| 8 voices + module, as measured in the spike's `.pool` (with its 64-key ring and 128-sample mix buffer) | 7,524 | |

- **The spike could not use the RAM region.** The link overflowed it by 8,308 B, and the region keeps a 4 KB headroom
  rule. The pool had 9.5 KB free, so the spike lowered the pool margin to 1 KB for that measurement build only.
- **A real module takes its voices from the engine arena** (344,628 B per patch) like any module's instances, so RAM is
  not a gate.
- **The voice could shrink to about 580 B.** The envelope keeps its rates and levels as `int[4]`, and bytes would do
  (−144 B per voice).

## Patch record and SysEx voices

Today a module record is a type byte, a parameter count, and one i32 (Q16.16) per stored parameter. The patch limit is
3,840 B ([D-256](../../docs/ledger/patch-format.md#D-256)). The p1 and p2 routing masks are at most 32 bits
(`maskBytes`), so cables can reach only a type's first 32 parameters.

| design | record | share of 3,840 | morph B side | notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| (a) 145 parameters as i32 | 582 B | 15 % | +599 B | Every parameter can be a card row. Cables reach only the first 32 parameters without a mask format change. Costs ≈ 5.5 KB of flash for defs. |
| (b) the voice as data: DX7's own 128-byte packed voice (`ndata`) plus ≈ 6 parameters (level, transpose, voice number, …) | ≈ 154 B | 4 % | +24 B (the blob is not morphed) | The patch carries a real DX7 voice byte for byte, and SysEx goes in and out unchanged. Single voice parameters are not cable targets, though a few can be exposed as parameters. |
| (b') as (b) with the 155-byte unpacked voice | ≈ 181 B | 5 % | | Simpler to edit, 27 B more. |

- **32 SysEx voices are 4,096 B** (a DX7 bulk dump is 4,104 B with its header and checksum). That is more than a whole
  patch (3,840 B), so a bank cannot live in a patch.
- **Options for a bank:**
  - (1) A cartridge store in the bank's data window, like the pattern store: 4,096 B plus a header is two 4 KB
    sectors per cartridge, taken from the patch slots' sectors or the factory region (16,253 B free there). Selecting
    voice n copies 128 B into the module's record.
  - (2) Only the editor keeps banks, and the FM-1 holds one voice per module. That costs no flash and no firmware code.

  (2) is the cheap start, and (1) can follow. Yamaha's ROM voices are not ours to ship, so factory cartridges would be
  our own voices.

## Per-operator jacks

| idea | memory | CPU | code |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Six operator outputs** | Each output is a 128-sample signal buffer of 512 B in the arena: 3 KB per voice, 24 KB for 8 voices. That is 48 of the 1,023 buffers. | One extra store per operator per sample, about +5 % of a voice [estimate]. | ≈ 300 B: each operator also writes its own buffer, Q24 to Q16. |
| **Six inputs that replace an operator** | None: they read the cables' buffers. | Cheaper than the sine it replaces: the input × the envelope gain. Modulation from the input works sample by sample inside the module. | ≈ 300 to 400 B in `FmCore::render`: an operator with a cable takes the input instead of `compute*`. |

Two limits:

- **Feedback through outside modules stays one 128-sample block late**, as #1220 found. Inside the module, operator
  feedback (algorithms 4 and 6) is exact.
- **The outputs are the expensive part in polyphony.** Exposing them only to a monophonic or paraphonic patch, or on a
  module with a voice count set low, would keep the arena cost down.

## Recommendation

1. **Proceed only after the owner rules on flash.** The wavetable mips (25,600 B) are the one option that leaves margin.
   Without such a ruling, the fallback stays as #1220 describes: an Algorithm module built from the existing
   operators, with no inter-operator feedback (algorithms 4 and 6).
2. **If go, build the module like this:**
   - a C port of msfa's core, using `SIN_T` and `EXP2_T`;
   - the voice stored as DX7's 128-byte packed record in the module's data, design (b);
   - banks kept in the editor first;
   - the operator inputs first, because they are cheap; the operator outputs as an option for low voice counts;
   - CPU at about 116 µs per voice, so a default of 8 voices (32 % of a block) leaves room for effects.
3. **Model cost.** Add an engine factor of about 0.70 for the new type to `cpu-costs.json` from this measurement, and
   re-measure the real module.

## Reproducing

- Host: `clang++ -std=c++11 -O2 [-DMSFA_OWN_TABLES] spike/dexed/core/*.cc spike/dexed/host_render.cc
  [build/dexed-spike/out/fw_tables.o]`.
- Model: `clang --target=riscv32-unknown-elf -march=rv32imf -mabi=ilp32 -O2 -ffunction-sections -fno-builtin
  -ffreestanding -nostdlib -fuse-ld=lld -Wl,-T,tools/cpu/link.ld -x c++ -std=c++11 -nostdinc++ -fno-exceptions -fno-rtti
  -fno-threadsafe-statics [-DMSFA_OWN_TABLES] spike/dexed/core/*.cc spike/dexed/rv32_bench.cc [-x none fw_tables_rv.o]`
  (with `fw_tables.c` compiled the same way for the shared build), then `node spike/dexed/rv32_run.mjs ELF`.
- JieLi and firmware: push `spike/dexed-1338`, or dispatch the `dexed-spike` workflow.
- Rig: `DXS_SPIKE=1` firmware from the CI artifact `dexed-spike` (folder `dxs/`), `emu:boot` first, then blocktime as
  above.

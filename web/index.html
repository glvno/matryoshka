    FM-1 & MIDI

    FM-1 connection
    Not connected·

    Send patch edits, knobs and notes to the FM-1.

    Sending a patch keeps its current sound. Saved assembly presets, including nested ones, transfer separately to the FM-1’s preset store. PRESET selects presets for matching device pages. Nested assemblies have no separate device page in this patch.

    Follow the FM-1

    Its keys, knobs and buttons play this page, whatever firmware it runs (only what that firmware sends over MIDI).

    MIDI keyboard MIDI learn (this patch)

    A row’s ⋮ menu or Commands’ MIDI learn… waits for a knob, a fader, a pad’s axis or the pitch bend wheel of a MIDI controller, or one of its notes; the first that moves drives the row (a note at once). A learned note flips a switch row each time it is played and sets any other row by how hard it is played; it no longer plays a note in this patch. Low and High set where the control’s ends land in the row’s range (%). A learned control no longer does its usual job (CC 1 no longer moves Main’s mod wheel). When the controller also sent a CC with a job of its own while learn waited, the status line and this list say so (“CC 16 also sent: turns K1”). CC 80..86 and 120..127 cannot be learned, nor anything from an FM-1. The patch keeps what it learned, and the FM-1 plays it on its own for every row its pages show. On the FM-1, hold HOME and press SEL for learn mode, turn a row’s knob, then move the control.

    Pads

    Pads are learned on the FM-1: HOME + SEL, then a drum key, then hit the pad. The map belongs to the FM-1, not to a patch: it plays the drum track that takes the keys, in any kit.

    Tempo

    Global: every patch plays at one tempo, and opening another patch keeps it. Main’s tempo row sets it. Patch: each patch plays its own tempo. Either way each patch keeps its own. A connected FM-1 shares this setting (GLO, PANEL, K4). MIDI clock in overrides both.

    The bar for play once, REC’s count-in and the arpeggiator’s bar rates; the tempo counts quarter notes in every one. It belongs to this browser and the FM-1, not to a patch. A connected FM-1 shares it (GLO, PANEL, then ALGO to its second page). Older FM-1 firmware plays 4/4.

    MIDI clock out

    Clock at the tempo (the global one, or Main’s with Tempo: Patch), and Start / Stop with Play (Space): several FM-1s play in time. An FM-1 follows it with CLOCK IN on (GLO, K2; on unless you switched it off). The clock stops when this page closes.

    Name & USB playback

    Your MIDI and audio apps will see “Matryoshka” followed by this name. Use up to 16 English letters, numbers, spaces or punctuation. Leave it blank for “Matryoshka”. Unplug and reconnect USB, or restart the FM-1, after saving to apply its new name.

    Hear playback from your phone or computer through the FM-1’s headphones or speaker. This level is separate from MASTER and takes effect when you save. Playback stays out of your USB recording. On the FM-1: GLO → SYSTEM, then ALGO to its second page → USB RETURN.

    Master EQ

    Shapes what the FM-1’s speaker and headphone jack play, for every patch, so a sound made on headphones still works through speakers in a room. It belongs to the FM-1, not to a patch, and stays out of USB recordings. On the FM-1: GLO, then ALGO to EQ.

    Each band you turn away from 0 dB costs CPU on every patch: about 3.5 % for the first and 1.3 % for each one after it. A patch’s own CPU estimate leaves this out. The heaviest patches, with every effect on and all three bands in use, may drop a voice.

    Which FM-1?

    More than one FM-1 answered. Choose the one the editor links to. This browser remembers it by its serial and links to it straight away next time; Choose FM-1… in FM-1 & MIDI asks again. The test rig is never linked without asking.
